# Slide decks

Decks are for talking, not reading. If a slide makes sense without the speaker, it probably wanted to be an email.

## Structure

- Open with the one thing the audience should remember, as a punch slide.
- One idea per slide. If a slide needs the word "and" in its title, it's probably two slides.
- End with a slide that says what happens next, not "Questions?"
- Most decks are 8 to 15 slides.

## Punch slides

A punch slide is a single sentence, set large.

- Size the text so the sentence fills the slide in two or three evenly filled lines.
- Never leave a short word like "the" or "a" stranded on its own line. Make the text smaller before you let that happen.
- No bullets, no logo, no footer on a punch slide.
- Put any aside in parentheses on a second, much smaller line.

## Bullet slides

- Three bullets at most. Five is a document.
- Each bullet is one line at presentation size.
- Bullets are phrases, not sentences, and they start with the important word.

## Type and color

- Titles use `--font-display` in `--crust`.
- Body text uses `--font-body` in `--ink` on `--flour`.
- Use `--primary` for one thing per slide, the thing you're pointing at.
- Dark decks use the dark theme values from tokens.css, never a separate palette.

## Images on slides

- Use figures from the figure library before asking for a new one.
- A figure gets the whole slide. Don't shrink it to fit next to bullets.
- Product photos use the same photos as the website.

## Speaker notes

Every slide has speaker notes with what to say, in the voice from voice.md. The notes are where the details go so the slide can stay short.
